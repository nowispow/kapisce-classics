“Come here, child,” cried her father as she appeared. “I have sent for

you an offer of marriage. Is it true?”

Elizabeth replied that it was.

“Very well--and this offer of marriage you have refused?”

“I have, sir.”

Mr. Collins's final speech, with its formal withdrawal and paradoxical resignation, showcases Austen's mastery of mock-heroic style. His elaborate justification—claiming he withdraws to avoid burdening the family with intercession—preserves his dignity through self-serving rhetorical flourishes while revealing his complete lack of genuine feeling for Elizabeth.
