## Footnotes and Analyses

<Callout title="The Etiquette of Introduction" variant="summary">
  Austen uses Chapter 2 to satirize the absurdity of Regency social laws. While the Bennets have lived near Mr. Bingley for weeks, they technically do not 'know' him until the patriarch pays a visit. This ritual was the 'API' of 19th-century social interaction—without the correct call, the data (friendship/marriage) could not flow.
</Callout>

<Callout title="Mary vs. Elizabeth: Genuine vs. Performative Wit" variant="explanation">
  This chapter draws a sharp line between Mary and Elizabeth. Mary tries to 'adjust her ideas' into academic extracts, while Elizabeth’s wit is reactive and natural. Austen suggests that true intelligence isn't found in 'great books' alone, but in the quickness of parts and understanding of character.
</Callout>
